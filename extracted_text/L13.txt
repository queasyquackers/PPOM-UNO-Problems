=== PAGE 3 ===
As physicians after we gather a history and perform an appropriate 
physical exam based on the reason for the patient seeking care we 
must: 
•
Analyze the findings to identify potential causes for the patient’s 
symptoms using clinical reasoning
•
The potential causes become our Differential Diagnosis (DDX)
•
List starting with most likely and including other plausible 
options that explain the patient symptoms
•
List may include diagnoses that have serious consequences if 
missed. 
•
Example: Patient with acute chest pain radiating to the left 
shoulder should be thought of as an myocardial infarction until 
proven otherwise because of the potential lethality if not 
diagnosed and treated immediately

=== PAGE 24 ===
Assessment-Diagnosis
•
From a documentation standpoint, you will list your diagnoses in the assessment 
section of the note.
•
The diagnosis may be very straight forward or more challenging and require 
further information to get to a final diagnosis if there are 2 possible causes for 
the patient’s condition. 
•
You always want to be specific as the data will allow. 
•
Example: subarachnoid hemorrhage, unspecified vs subarachnoid 
hemorrhage, left temporal lobe

=== PAGE 25 ===
Planning the Diagnostic and Treatment Strategy
•
The Plan of Care should be a logical flow from your working diagnoses listed 
in the assessment section of your note. 
•
The Plan should include:
•
Laboratory tests to allow you differentiate between 2 potential 
diagnoses or guide your therapeutic management of a patient. 
•
Diagnostic interventions to help you rule in or rule out a diagnosis. 
•
Patient education
•
Medication changes
•
Referrals 
•
Follow up visits

Vaccination status: up to date 
PSH: none

SH: lives at home with mom, dad, and older sister; goes to Happy hills daycare during 
week, no pets in home, not exposed to smokers in the home
Meds: none

Plan Example
1. Perform tympanogram to assess for potential hearing involvement
2. Start on Amoxicillin 90 mg/kg/d x 10 days
3. Discussed repopulating GI tract with healthy bacteria and recommended feeding child 
yogurt once daily while on antibiotics at least 2 hours after medication to help with this.
4. Discussed importance of keeping child hydrated and discussed warning signs including 
decrease in # wet diapers to follow up immediately or call the office
5. OMT was performed to the above regions using MFR and cranial. Patient tolerated 
procedure well. 
6. Patient to f/u if symptoms do not start to improve in 48-72 hours.

resolution of middle ear fluid and or recheck ear exam.
