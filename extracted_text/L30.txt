Lower motor neurons
versus 
Upper motor neurons 
Nerves and ganglia are in PNS.
Tracts and nuclei are in CNS.
Lower motor neurons (also termed “motoneurons”) innervate 
skeletal muscle.
    Cells bodies in spinal cord or cranial nerve nuclei.
     Their tracts rarely decussate.      
      Their nerves never decussate.
Upper motor neurons have cell bodies at the cerebral cortex 
and brainstem. These project to lower motor neurons of the 
brainstem and spinal cord, not to muscles. 
 
Variety of projection patterns: some decussate, some 
 
don’t, and some project bilaterally.

The two paths of the corticospinal tract
“Body-representation” fibers descend from cerebral cortex.
At the lower medulla, 80-85% fibers decussate and form the
lateral corticospinal tract.
15-20% of fibers do not cross and descend ipsilaterally as 
the ventral corticospinal tract. Most of these decussate in 
the spinal cord.
Thereby, cerebral cortex controls the contralateral body.

• Stroke, spinal cord injury, multiple sclerosis, cerebral palsy

Corticobulbar tract
Bilaterally innervates cranial nuclei controlling jaw, tongue, 
larynx, pharynx, and scm/upper trapezius. 
EXCEPT: Fibers projecting to facial nucleus show 
distinctive pattern:  
Contralateral nucleus receives innervation from whole 
corticobulbar tract (upper and lower face).
Ipsilateral facial nucleus only receives innervation for 
controlling upper face.
No redundancy for lower face. No compensation for loss.
Input to all other nuclei is bilateral though hypoglossal has 
more from contralateral cerebral cortex, i.e. partial 
compensation with tongue control.

=== PAGE 32 ===
Summary
•
The corticospinal tract arises from the precentral gyrus (mostly M1) and 
rostral adjacent cortex (PMC and SMA) and projects to the contralateral spinal 
cord.
•
The corticobulbar tract arises from the same areas and projects bilaterally to 
the cranial nerve nuclei, excepting the facial nucleus.
•
Upper motor neuron damage can be expressed as weakness and spastic 
paralysis whereas lower motor neuron damage is expressed flaccid paralysis.
•
Different descending brainstem tracts have different projection patterns.
•
Damage above and below the red nucleus leads to decorticate and 
decerebrate rigidity, respectively.
