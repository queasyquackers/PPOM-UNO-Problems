•

•

interneurons (GABA/Gly)

•
Brainstem (GABA): risus sardonicus (sardonic

raised eyebrow and open grin

•

Flexor Withdrawal Reflex
Sequence
1. Painful stimulus on hand
2. Pain sensor is excited
3. Sensory information is conveyed to the spinal cord
4. Usually pass the information to the interneurons to 
1) excite flexor motor neurons of the arm, and 
2) inhibit extensor motor neurons of the arm, 
withdrawing the limb from stimulus
5. The sensory neuron could also synapse with the 
2nd order neurons to transmit pain signal
• More complex reflex 
– Interneurons
– Multiple muscles
• Neurotransmitters:
Glu, ACh, Gly

Descending Pathway for 
Somatic Motor Activity
Corticospinal Tract
-
Voluntary movement: upper motor 
neurons (UMNs)
-
Start in motor cortex (precentral gyrus of 
the frontal lobe) and descend through the 
internal capsule and brainstem 
-
Most of the fibers cross the midline in the 
pyramidal decussation (at the 
spinomedullary junction) and form the 
lateral corticospinal tract (lateral funiculus) 
-
Synapse at the contralateral spinal cord 
ventral horn to activate lower motor 
neurons (LMNs)
From Nolte’s Essentials of the Human Brain, 2nd ed, 2019

=== PAGE 37 ===
General Rules of Lateralization
For descending (motor) tracts:
Lesions before the decussation level cause 
contralateral deficits.
Lesions after the decussation level cause 
ipsilateral deficits.
For ascending (sensory) tracts:
Lesions before the decussation level cause 
ipsilateral deficits.
Lesions after the decussation level cause 
contralateral deficits.

=== PAGE 38 ===
General Rules of Lateralization
- my simplified version
Lesions below the decussation level cause 
ipsilateral deficits.
Lesions above the decussation level cause 
contralateral deficits.
This is true for both ascending and descending 
tracts. 
• For ascending tracts, before decussation = 
below decussation
• For descending tracts, before decussation = 
above decussation
