Functions of the Limbic System
The limbic system is involved in primordial emotion, memory, 
olfaction, attention, feeding and mating behavior. (5 F’s - 
Feeding, Fleeing, Fighting, Feeling, and Sex)
• Establishes emotional states
• Links conscious functions of cerebral cortex with autonomic 
functions of the brainstem
• Facilitates memory storage and retrieval

Intrinsic Organization of the Hippocampus
The hippocampus has four sectors/ areas: CA1, 
CA2, CA3, CA4 (CA = Cornu Ammonis)
CA1 is adjacent to the subiculum and CA4 lies 
within the hilus of the dentate gyrus
Efferent fibers of the dentate gyrus synapse in 
CA3 pyramidal cells
Pyramidal cells in area CA1 are exceptionally 
sensitive to oxygen deprivation
Cells die within minutes without a supply of fresh 
arterial blood

Temporal Lobe Epilepsy
Temporal Lobe Epilepsy
• The temporal lobe (especially the hippocampus and 
amygdala) has a lower threshold for epileptic seizure 
activity than the other cortical areas
• Seizures that originate in these regions are called 
complex partial (psychomotor) seizures
• Temporal lobe epilepsy may include abnormal 
sensations, repeated involuntary movements such as 
chewing, swallowing, and lip smacking, disorders of 
consciousness, memory loss and hallucinations

Amygdala and Emotions
• Amygdala plays a pivotal role in emotions and 
drives: Fear
• Amygdala is an active participant in all major limbic 
functions 
• Reciprocal connections between Amygdala-
Hypothalamus mediate changes in heart rate, 
peristalsis, piloerection, sweating, and other changes 
seen with strong emotions
• It plays an important role in attaching emotional 
significance to memories
• Olfactory tract projections to amygdala may 
explain the occasional ability of odors to evoke 
emotions

=== PAGE 22 ===
Kluver-Bucy Syndrome
Bilateral lesions of the amygdala
These lesions result in:
Placidity: decrease in aggressive behavior
Psychic blindness: objects in visual field are treated 
inappropriately.  Example: Monkeys approach snake 
with inappropriate docility
Hypermetamorphosis: visual stimuli are repeatedly 
approached as though they were completely new
Hyper-orality: Increased oral exploratory behavior
Hypersexuality and loss of sexual preference
Anterograde amnesia: deficit in forming new memories

Alzheimer’s Disease
• Gray matter atrophy, hippocampal 
atrophy and ventricle enlargement
• Significant decrease in ACh
• Significant degeneration of basal 
nucleus
• ACh antagonist produce deficiencies in 
memory tasks reversed by AChE 
Inhibitor
• AChE Inhibitor improves memory and 
attention in patients with Alzheimer’s
• AChE Inhibitor also has been shown to 
decrease hippocampal atrophy
