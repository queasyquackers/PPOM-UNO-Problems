Injuries to the superior trunk (C5-C6) of the brachial plexus (Erb-Duchenne Palsy):
Clinical signs of damage to upper trunk:
1. Loss of lateral rotation of arm
2. Loss of flexion of forearm and supination impaired
3. Pronated forearm, medially rotated arm, and extended forearm due to unopposed 
action of pronators, pectoralis major m., and triceps brachii m., respectively, result in
"waiter's tip position".
4. Sensory deficits on lateral aspect of shoulder, arm, forearm, and hand.

Damage to inferior trunk of brachial plexus (C8-T1): Klumpke's Palsy
Clinical signs of Klumpke's Palsy:
1. Loss of all lumbrical and interosseous mm. results in loss of abduction and addiction of fingers;        
hyperextension of MCP joints due to unopposed action of extensor digitorum m.
2. Loss of all lumbrical and interosseous mm. results in flexion of PIP joints in digits 2-5 due to unopposed 
action of intact flexor digitorum superficialis m. Flexion of DIP joints in digits 1-2 depends on functionality of
the lateral side of flexor digitorum profundus mm. and severity of injury.
3. Total claw hand results from loss of all intrinsic hand muscles innervated by C8-T1 fibers from ulnar and 
median nn.
4. Radial deviation in wrist flexion from loss of action of flexor carpi ulnaris m.
5. Loss of cutaneous innervation of medial arm and medial forearm and medial hand
Moore Clinically Oriented Anatomy 7 th ed., Figs. B6.12F, 6.18

Summary of different claw effects from ulnar n. lesions at different levels
1. Klumpke's Palsy (damage to inferior trunk of brachial plexus)
-All four MCP joints extended due to loss of all intrinsic muscles of the hand from C8-T1 fibers from
median and ulnar nn. and unopposed action of extensor digitorum m.
-PIP joints of digits 2-3 flexed due to unopposed action of flexor digitorum superficialis, DIP joint 2-3 flexion
depends on functionality of lateral half of flexor digitorum profundus and severity of injury. 
-PIP of digits 4-5 flexed because of unopposed action of FDS, DIP not flexed because no action from
medial side of FDP.
-Result: total claw hand, digits 2-3 possibly clawed worse. 
2. High lesion of ulnar n. (i.e. at elbow region, broken humerus or cubital tunnel syndrome)
-MCP of digits 4-5 extended because of loss of interosseous mm. and medial two lumbrical mm.
-MCP of digits 2-3 not extended because lateral two lumbrical mm. are still intact.
-PIP and DIP of digits 2-3 not flexed because lateral lumbrical mm. are still intact.
-PIP and DIP of digits 4-5 same as above. PIP flexed, DIP not flexed.
-Result: "ulnar paradox", higher lesion (=more serious) than wrist lesion, but claw effect is lessened due to 
lack of action from lateral side of FDP. Claw effect will worsen as nerve heals and action of FDP is restored.
3. Low lesion of ulnar n. (i.e. at wrist, Guyon's canal (ulnar tunnel) entrapment)
-Same as above, except both PIP and DIP of digits 4-5 are flexed because of unopposed action of FDP. 
-Result: Ulnar claw
Moore Clinically Oriented Anatomy 7 th ed., Fig. B6.12F
