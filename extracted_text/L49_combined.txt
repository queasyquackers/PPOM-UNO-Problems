ScholarRx
• Dyssomnias – disorders of sleep-wake time and/or quality
• Primary (intrinsic)
• Primary insomnia
• Sleep-related movement disorders, e.g., restless legs syndrome
• Central disorders of hypersomnolence, e.g., narcolepsy
• Secondary (extrinsic)
• Secondary insomnia, e.g., depression, anxiety
• Circadian rhythm sleep-wake disorders, e.g., jet-lag
• Obstructive sleep apnea, congestive heart failure, GERD, acute illness, etc.
• Parasomnias – physical behaviors (e.g., sleepwalking) or experiences (e.g., 
sleep terrors) that occur during or around sleep

Resp Med 2025; C1B; S1B
• Obstructive sleep apnea
• Mechanical obstruction by excess soft tissues resulting in rapid, repetitive
airway collapse and hypooxygenation
• Risk: increased age, obesity, large tonsils/neck, PCOD, hypothyroidism, genetic predisposition, SHF , CKF , 
alcohol, smoking, male
• Prevalence: 32% in U.S. adults > 20 years; 59% male, 41% female

• Diagnosis: Polysomnogram
• Treatment: 
• Continue positive airway pressure (CPAP) is treatment of choice

--- Page 21 ---
C1B; S1B
• Sleepwalking aka somnambulism
• Despite being physiologically asleep, the person appears awake and 
performs activities and have no memory of the event the next morning
• Occurs in up to 30% of the US population as a single, non-repeating event. 
• Chronic sleepwalking may affect up to 2%
• Usually resolves spontaneously in children
• Treatment in adults: safety, treat underlying medical or psychological 
conditions, benzodiazepines
Parasomnias (N3)
--- Page 22 ---
S1B
• REM Sleep Behavior Disorder
• Acting out dreams by yelling, kicking, punching, grabbing, or 
jumping
• Usually older persons who remember the dreams, but not 
their behavior while sleeping
• Polysomnogram shows REM sleep despite lack of atonia
• Treatment: melatonin or low-dose clonazepam
• Brainstem histology shows Lewy bodies that may be 
associated with Parkinson’s, multisystem atrophy and Lewy 
body dementia

• Summary
1. Normal sleep physiology is dictated by circadian rhythms 
that orchestrate hormonal and physiologic functions that 
maintain normal homeostasis
2. Sleep disorders are prevalent and many can be diagnosed 
by a primary provider with sleep studies while some 
require outside consultation for management
3. Sleep is an integral part of physician wellness that optimizes 
safety and care of the patient
