=== PAGE 7 ===
Anatomy Overview 
•
Thalamus is the largest 
component of diencephalon, lies 
between brainstem & cerebral 
hemisphere
•
Almost all thalamic nuclei have 
rich reciprocal connections with 
cerebral cortex
•
Thalamus is divided into 3 
principal nuclear masses (anterior, 
medial, lateral) by the internal 
medullary lamina (also contains 
intralaminar nuclei)
•
Thin reticular nucleus lies on 
lateral aspect of thalamus
Crossman, A. R., & Neary, D. (2015). Neuroanatomy: an illustrated colour text. 

=== PAGE 8 ===
Functional Overview
 
•
Nuclei that transmit general and special 
sensory information corresponding to 
regions of sensory cortices
•
Nuclei that receive impulses from cerebellum 
and basal ganglia and interface with motor 
regions of frontal lobe
•
Nuclei that have connections with associate 
and limbic areas of cerebral cortex 
Crossman, A. R., & Neary, D. (2015). Neuroanatomy: an illustrated colour text. 
New York: Churchill Livingstone Elsevier. 

=== PAGE 9 ===
Inputs to Cortex
Cerebral cortex receives 
inputs via:
1) Thalamus sends specific 
information to restricted 
cortical areas via relay 
nuclei
 
2) Diffuse, modulatory 
inputs that set the general 
level of excitability of broad 
cortical areas via  
association nuclei

=== PAGE 10 ===
Afferent vs. Efferent Pathways
A fundamental difference 
between pathways entering 
the cortex and pathways 
leaving the cortex:
Efferent pathways (e.g., the 
corticospinal, corticobulbar 
and corticopontine tracts) 
can go directly from the 
cortex to their destinations. 
They DO NOT go through 
the thalamus

Thalamocortical Pathways
Largely similar circuits:
1) Start – activation of peripheral
sensory receptors (1st order 
neurons)
2) Intermediate centers – e.g.
Brainstem (2nd order neurons)

nucleus (3rd order neurons)
4) Neocortex – specific region &
specific layers (conscious 
perception)

Schiff et al. 2007
• 38 yo right-handed man sustained a closed head injury following an 
assault
• Suffered blunt trauma to right frontal region → bilateral subdural 
hematomas (right > left).
• Initial Glasgow Coma Scale: 3 (deep coma)
• Right temporal craniotomy was performed → evacuation of hematoma
• Post-op patient remained behaviorally unresponsive for at least 2 
months
Background
Findings
• Deep brain stimulation (DBS) of thalamus produced acute changes in arousal including
–
Increased heart rate, well sustained eye opening & rapid bilateral head turning to a voice
• On day ~143 patient showed first instances of functional object use and intelligible verbalization 
( no previous episodes were observed on previous 33 evaluations/6 mo.)
• Behavioral improvements temporally coincided with onset of DBS and did not emerge until 160 
days after initiation of rehabilitation program, indicating they were partially attributable to DBS.
