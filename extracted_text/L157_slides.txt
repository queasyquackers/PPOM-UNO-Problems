• The drivers of cancer growth are the oncogenic pathways in malignant cells, the 
tumor microenvironment, and malignant cells’ escape from host immune 
surveillance.
• Targeted anticancer therapeutics specifically act on well-defined extracellular or 
intracellular molecules to interrupt the oncogenic signaling, tumor growth, and 
proliferation. 
• The targeted therapies are usually used in combination regimens including with 
nonselective cytotoxic chemotherapies and/or other targeted drugs.
Approach to cancer treatment
Cancer treatment has evolved since the recognition of cancer genetics 
and the development of targeted therapeutics that specifically attack 
cancer cells.

Targets of biologic and small molecule targeted agents:
Small molecule drugs and Biologics
• Small molecule drugs block intracellular targets, preventing activation of 
signaling pathways. 
• Antibody therapeutics block soluble growth factors or specific cell surface 
receptors, preventing activation of the receptor’s signaling pathway.
• Antibody-drug conjugates consist of an antibody that carries a cytotoxic payload 
to tumors overexpressing specific growth factor receptors.
• Immune checkpoint inhibitors unleash the immune system against the tumors.
• CAR-T cells are genetically engineered T cells against specific tumor antigens.
8
Targeted agents block specific molecules in oncogenic pathways that lead to 
dysregulated cancer cell growth and survival, which interferes with growth, 
progression, and spread of cancer.

===== PAGE 17 =====
Tyrosine Kinase Signaling: NRTK and RTK
17
Non-receptor (soluble) tyrosine kinase (NRTK)
NRTKs are not connected to transmembrane domains. 
NRTKs phosphorylate tyrosine residues on target proteins important in the regulation of a 
vast array of cellular functions, such as gene expression, immune response, cell survival, 
division.
Receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK)
RTKs are cell surface receptors for many polypeptide growth factors, cytokines, and 
hormones. 
Ligand binding to the extracellular domain induces homo- and hetero-dimerization of the 
receptors, autophosphorylation on intracellular tyrosine residues, and activation of 
intracellular pathways. 
Growth factors, like cytokines, are soluble messengers that bind cell surface receptors, which 
initiates transmembrane and intracellular cascade of events in signal transduction.

===== PAGE 18 =====
Tyrosine Kinase Signaling: EGFR, VEGFR and VEGF
18
Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)
EGFR belongs to the RTK family. EGFR is activated by epidermal growth factor and 
transforming growth factor-. 
Mutations resulting in overexpression of EGFR lead to continuous activation of downstream 
signaling pathways, uncontrolled cell proliferation and survival, and tumorigenesis. EGFR 
overexpression is found in numerous cancers and is associated with poor prognosis.
Vascular endothelial growth factor receptors (VEGFRs)
The three types of VEGFRs (-1, -2, -3) that regulate angiogenesis (new blood vessel 
formation) and vasculogenesis (formation of the circulatory system). Vascular endothelial 
growth factor-A (VEGF-A) is a member of the VEGF family and primary regulator of 
angiogenesis. It stimulates proliferation, migration, and survival of endothelial cells. VEGF-A 
is also important for vasculogenesis from endothelial precursor cells during embryonic 
development.
Abnormal angiogenesis occurs in cancer, age-related macular degeneration, and ischemic 
stroke.

Mechanisms: Receptor Tyrosine Kinase Inhibitors (RTKIs)
21
Small molecules Passively diffuse into cytosol
Nomenclature: All have the suffix “tinibs” or “nibs”
Mechanism Block the intracellular ATP binding sites of the tyrosine kinase domain, 
preventing autophosphorylation and activation of signaling pathways
Oral
CYP3A4 substrates
Oral route and once or twice daily dosing is convenient for the patient.
High potential for drug-drug interactions. Many are also substrates of P-gp.
Adverse effects Mechanism related: Inhibition of growth pathways in normal cells
• Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
• Rash (acneiform) / desquamation (skin peeling)
• Liver injury
• Myocardial dysfunction / heart failure
• Bone marrow dysfunction: anemia, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia
• Susceptibility to infection
• Hypertension
• Periorbital edema
• and more

BCR-ABL Kinase Inhibitors
For the treatment of chronic myeloid leukemia (CML) 
24
BCR::ABL1 fusion oncogene – the Philadelphia chromosome – is a clonal cytogenetic 
abnormality resulting from a translocation between chromosomes 9 and 22. It codes a 
constitutively active cytoplasmic (NRTK) tyrosine kinase, BCR::ABL1, that drives unregulated 
cell growth.  
The second-generation inhibitors (all those developed after imatinib) are more potent 
than imatinib and are effective against some resistance-conferring mutations.
Tumor cells harboring the T315I mutation is susceptible only to ponatinib and asciminib 
due to different mechanisms of action. (T315I: threonine to isoleucine point mutation)
Imatinib • Dasatinib • Nilotinib
Ponatinib • Bosutinib • Asciminib

===== PAGE 72 =====
• Immune checkpoint inhibitors are drugs that prevent the checkpoint proteins from 
downregulating T cell actions, thereby unleashing the T cell response against the 
tumor cells. Targets are CTLA-4, PD-1 and PD-L1. Toxicities include autoimmune 
reactions due to T cell activation and proliferation and infusion hypersensitivity 
reactions.
• CAR-T cells – chimeric antigen receptor T cells – are genetically engineered to 
express artificial T cell receptors targeting specific tumor antigens. Both the antigen-
binding and T cell activating functions are combined in one receptor. CAR-T cells are 
used in the treatment of CD19-positive leukemias / lymphomas and BCMA-positive 
multiple myeloma. Potentially life-threatening toxicities include severe cytokine 
release syndrome, an inflammatory syndrome with fever and multiorgan failure, and 
immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome (ICANS), which is a 
neuropsychiatric syndrome characterized by encephalopathy, hallucinations, and 
language dysfunction.

• Rituximab targets what antigen on what types of immune cells?
CD20 cell surface protein expressed on 95% of B cells in peripheral blood and secondary lymphoid 
organs. CD20 is absent on terminally differentiated plasma cells.
• What is rituximab’s mechanism of tumor and autoimmune cell eradication?
Rituximab induces antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity and complement-dependent cytotoxicity.
• Patients should be screened for what infections prior to beginning rituximab tx?
All patients should be screened for HBV infection. In high-risk populations, screen for latent 
infections, including tuberculosis, hepatitis C virus, and HIV. 
• What is the purpose of administering rituximab in conjunction with hyaluronidase?
Hyaluronidase increases permeability of subcutaneous tissue through temporary depolymerization 
of hyaluronan (high mw polysaccharide in extracellular matrix), which increases the dispersion and 
absorption rate of the drug.
• How do CDK4/6 inhibitors and PARP inhibitors cause tumor cell death?
CDK4/6 inhibitors prevent release of Rb tumor suppressor protein, which prevents progression from 
G1 to S cell cycle phase, leading to cell cycle arrest. PARP inhibitors block DNA damage response 
(DDR) pathways in patients with deleterious BRCA1/2 tumors, which prevents tumor cells from 
repairing DNA, making them vulnerable to apoptosis.

• What is the mechanistic concept of the immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)?
Immune checkpoints are inhibitory signals the limit T cell activation. Blocking immune checkpoints 
on cytotoxic (CD8+) T cells allows them to recognize cancer cells expressing antigens recognized 
by the T cells and eliminate them. 
• What types of cancers are the ICIs used for?
Many types of solid tumors.
• What are some serious and potentially life-threatening toxicities of the ICIs?
Immune-related adverse reactions and infusion reactions.
• What is the mechanistic concept of the CAR-T cell drugs?
CAR-T cells are genetically engineered to express artificial T cell receptors targeting specific tumor 
antigens. Both the antigen-binding and T cell activating functions are combined in one receptor. 
• What are some serious and potentially life-threatening toxicities of CAR T cells?
Cytokine release syndrome and immune effector cell neurotoxicity syndrome
