===== PAGE 5 =====
Key points: Pathogenic protozoa
• A wide variety of protozoal parasites can be transmitted to human hosts by insect vectors, directly from 
other mammalian reservoirs, of from one person to another. The immune system is critical in protecting 
against the pathological effects of infection. 
• Amoebiasis caused by Entamoeba histolytica, spread by the fecal-oral route, infects the intestines and 
can lead to amoebic dysentery or liver abscess.
• Giardia lamblia is transmitted through contaminated water and leads to diarrhea, cramping, and 
malabsorption.
• Trichomonas vaginalis is the most common nonviral sexually transmitted infection worldwide. Infections 
range from an acute severe inflammatory disease to asymptomatic carrier state. 
• African trypanosomiasis (sleeping sickness) is caused by Trypanosoma brucei spread by tsetse flies and 
can lead to CNS infection and coma. Chagas disease, caused by Trypanosoma cruzi and transmitted by 
triatomine bugs, can lead to severe heart and digestive disease. 
• Leishmania species transmitted by sandflies cause cutaneous / mucocutaneous and visceral disease. 
Visceral leishmaniasis is life-threatening.
• Toxoplasma gondii is an obligate intracellular parasite transmitted to humans from cats (the only 
definitive host), but more commonly via undercooked infected meat products. The infection is often 
asymptomatic but can cause serious complications in pregnant individuals and immunocompromised 
patients.
• Drug therapy treats infection and reduces transmission. Effective vaccines against protozoal infections 
are not available. 5

===== PAGE 6 =====
Key points (Starred drugs are in bold font.)
Antiprotozoal drugs target protozoal enzymes, metabolic processes, and organelles not present in humans 
and, therefore, are more selective for parasitic cells than for human cells. In addition, many protozoa need 
anaerobic or microaerophilic environments to function, whereas human cells require oxygen.
• The drugs interfere with protozoal nucleic acid synthesis, inhibit protein synthesis or metabolic 
processes, disrupt protozoal organelles and/or membrane integrity.
• Metronidazole, tinidazole, secnidazole, and nitazoxanole are nitroimidazole compounds active against 
anaerobic and microaerophilic parasites. They are converted by parasitic pyruvate:ferrodoxin 
oxidoreductase to toxic nitro free radical intermediates, which cause death of the parasite. Resistance, 
though uncommon, arises through decreased activation or increased inactivation of drug. 
• Metronidazole PK: several routes of administration, widely distributed, hepatic metabolism, 
bioavailability nearly 100%, and eliminated in urine/feces as active drug and metabolites. 
• Metronidazole is used in infants, children, and adults for the treatment of amoebiasis, giardiasis, 
trichomoniasis, and anaerobic bacterial infections. 
• Tinidazole is used orally or intravaginally for adults and children 3 years old for the same parasitic infections. 
• Secnidazole is a long-acting nitroimidazole labeled for oral single-dose treatment of bacterial vaginosis and vaginal 
trichomoniasis in patients 12 years old. 
• Nitazoxanide is a broad-spectrum antiparasitic agent used orally for the treatment of and cryptosporidiosis-
associated diarrhea, giardiasis, and other parasitic infections.
• Paromomycin is an aminoglycoside (protein synthesis inhibitor), which is not absorbed from the GI tract. 
It is administered orally for intestinal amebiasis and certain other luminal parasitic infections. 6

===== PAGE 7 =====
Key points: Trypanosomiasis
• Sleeping sickness, caused byTrypanasoma brucei, is endemic in some regions of sub -Saharan Africa. 
T. brucei gambiense causes human West African trypanosomiasis – gambiense HAT. It has an 
incubation period of weeks to months and is usually slowly progressive. T. brucei rhodesiense causes 
human East African trypanosomiasis – rhodesiense HAT. It has an incubation period of less than three 
weeks and is usually rapidly progressive within weeks. Gambiense HAT accounts for a larger burden of 
disease than rhodesiense HAT. 
• The approach to treatment selection is guided by patient age and weight, drug availability, neurological 
signs and symptoms, and CSF findings.
• Fexinidazole is an oral drug preferred for both gambiense and rhodesiense early -stage 
(hemolymphatic) HAT in children ≥6 years old, ≥20 kg. It is oral, safer than the other drugs, and crosses 
the blood-brain barrier. 
• Children <6 years old, <20 kg with early -stage gambiense HAT are treated with IM or IV pentamidine 
and, for rhodesiense HAT, with IV suramin.
• Late-stage (meningoencephalitic) gambiense HAT, nonsevere disease, is oral fexinidazole. Oral 
nifurtimox with IV eflornithine combination therapy (NECT) is more effective in severe disease and the 
preferred treatment.
• Late-stage rhodesiense HAT is treated with the arsenical melarsoprol, which is very toxic.

===== PAGE 8 =====
Key points
• Fexinidazole, like other nitroimidazoles, is activated by parasite nitroreductase to nitro free radicals that 
damage DNA and proteins (the proposed mechanism). PK: oral with the main meal of the day, widely 
distributed, extensively metabolized via CYPs to active metabolites, half -life between 9 and 27 hours. 
Adverse effects include neuropsychiatric effects, nausea/vomiting, headache, dizziness, tremor, 
neutropenia, and QT interval prolongation. Follow -up clinical evaluation should be performed at 6, 12, 
18, and 24 months after completion of therapy or at any time symptoms recur due to risk of recurrence, 
which has been observed in patients with severe late -stage gambiense HAT.
• Pentamidine isethionate impairs the parasite’s mitochondrial activity and interferes with polyamine 
synthesis, RNA polymerase, and ribosomal functions and prevents synthesis of proteins, nucleic acids, 
and phospholipids. PK: slow IV infusion; terminal elimination weeks to months. Common adverse effects 
include nausea, fatigue, hypoglycemia, and injection site pain and local abscess. Potentially serious 
toxicities include reversible bone marrow suppression, hepatoxicity, nephrotoxicity, and neuropathy.
• Nifurtimox is an oral trypanocidal imidazole compound taken with food for improved absorption, 
metabolized in the liver, excreted in urine as metabolites, and has a half -life of ~3 hours. Common 
adverse effects include nausea/vomiting, headache, dizziness, and polyneuropathy.
• Eflornithine is an irreversible inhibitor of ornithine decarboxylase, which rapidly depletes the parasites 
of polyamines. It is given by slow IV infusion and has a half -life of ~3 hours. Toxicities include fever, 
pruritis, hypertension, nausea/vomiting/diarrhea, headache, dizziness, seizure, and bone marrow 
suppression.

===== PAGE 9 =====
Key points
• Suramin is the preferred agent for early -stage rhodesiense HAT in children <6 years old, <20 kg. It forms 
stable complexes with proteins, interacting with a wide variety of biomolecules such as serine 
oligopeptidase, dihydrofolate reductase, thymidylate synthase, and several glycolytic enzymes, inhibiting 
energy metabolism in the parasite. It is given by slow IV infusion weekly over four weeks, does not 
penetrate the CSF, and has a very long half-life (is detectable in urine for 3 months). Early adverse 
effects include nausea, vomiting, and fatigue. Severe toxicities that occur over time include bone marrow 
toxicity, nephrotoxicity, peripheral nephropathy, chronic diarrhea, and more. Suramin is also active 
against the nematode (worm) Onchocerca volvulus that causes river blindness. Coinfected patients 
experience severe immunologic reactions. Therefore, coinfection should be excluded before 
administering suramin.
• Melarsoprol is the only agent effective against late -stage rhodesiense HAT. It is an arsenical prodrug 
converted to the active and extremely toxic melarsen oxide, which reacts avidly and reversibly with 
sulfhydryl groups. It forms a melarsen oxide-trypanothione complex that inhibits trypanothione reductase 
activity. Melarsen oxide inactivates most enzymes of both parasite and host. Melarsoprol reactive 
encephalopathy occurs in up to 18% of patients and is fatal in many of the cases. The drug is toxic to 
heart, liver, kidneys, and nerves among others.
9

===== PAGE 10 =====
Key points
• American trypanosomiasis – Chagas disease – is caused by transmission by the triatomine bug 
(Reduviidae family). It is treated with the oral imidazole compound benznidazole (preferred, more 
tolerable, twice daily dose) or nifurtimox (alternative, three times daily dose). They are reduced by 
parasitic nitroreductase to nitro free radicals that form covalent attachment to biomolecules. Both drugs 
are trypanocidal against trypomastigote and amastigore forms of T. cruzi. Treatment of acute disease 
reduces parasitemia, morbidity, and mortality. Cure rate is >80%, but with geographic variations. For 
chronic disease, cures are possible but the drugs are less effective than for acute disease. Adverse 
effects include nausea/vomiting, headache, dizziness, and polyneuropathy. 
• Leishmania is a trypanosomatid protozoa transmitted by the bite of certain species of sand fly. Diseases 
include cutaneous leishmaniasis (Old World and New World varieties) and visceral leishmaniasis (kala -
azar). Leishmania species differ in their sensitivity to chemotherapeutic agents.
• Sodium stibogluconate (most countries) and meglumine (French -speaking countries) are pentavalent 
antimonial agents widely used first -line agents. Pentavalent prodrugs are reduced to trivalent antimonial 
by parasitic reductase, which inhibits trypanothione reductase, which interferes with trypanothione redox 
system, inducing rapid efflux of trypanothione and glutathione from the cell and also inhibiting 
trypanothione reductase. PK: IV administration, drug is sequestered in tissues, terminal half -life of up to 
80 hours. The antimonials are fairly well tolerated. Adverse effects include dose -related muscle aches, 
nausea/vomiting, fatigue, and rash and nonspecific EKG changes, such as T wave suppression and QT 
interval prolongation. Additional potentially serious toxicities include bone marrow suppression, 
hepatotoxicity, nephrotoxicity, and pancreatitis. 10

===== PAGE 11 =====
Key points
• Amphotericin B liposomal IV is recommended in certain areas of India and Nepal where there is 
resistance to the antimonials. Amphotericin B has a high cure rate in the treatment of mucocutaneous and 
visceral leishmaniasis. Toxicities are infusion reactions (“shake and bake”), nephrotoxicity, anemia, 
hypersensitivity reactions, and more.
• Miltefosine is the first consistently effective and safe oral drug for visceral, mucosal, and cutaneous 
leishmaniasis, but efficacy varies significantly by species and by geography. Its mechanism is not known. 
It is also effective against free -living encephalitic amoeba, such as Naegleria fowleri. As a phospholipid, it 
may interact with phospholipids and steroids in parasitic cell membranes, inhibition of cytochrome c 
oxidase which inhibits mitochondrial function, and apoptosis -like death. Adverse effects are common, 
generally mild nausea/vomiting/diarrhea, anemia/decreased platelet count, increased serum 
aminotransferases, increased serum creatinine, headache, dizziness, rash, urticaria, SJS (rare). 
Contraindicated in all trimesters of pregnancy – teratogenic, fetotoxic. Breast feeding should be avoided. 
Reduced sperm count, mobility, and morphology, decreased ejaculate volume, scrotal pain and 
tenderness in males.

===== PAGE 12 =====
Key points
• Toxoplasmosis is an acute, self-limiting illness in most people, which does not require treatment. 
Immunocompetent persons with primary infection are usually asymptomatic. Toxoplamosis has a 
worldwide distribution.
• Pregnant patients are treated for acute acquired toxoplasmosis to prevent transmission to the fetus. 
Treatment at <14 weeks gestation is with spiramycin, an oral macrolide that concentrates in the 
placenta. If >18 weeks gestation and documented fetal infection, then pyrimethamine plus 
sulfadiazine plus leucovorin is administered. 
• HIV/AIDS patients with cerebral toxoplasmosis are treated with oral pyrimethamine plus sulfadiazine 
plus leucovorin or TMP-SMX IV, followed by suppressive therapy with pyrimethamine + sulfadiazine 
+ leucovorin. 
• Toxoplamosis prophylaxis in HIV/AIDS patients is oral TMP -SMX.

What are the medical justifications for using two different drugs in the treatment of amoebiasis? What are the 
drugs described in this PowerPoint?
• Metronidazole is a tissue-acting drug to eliminate invasive trophozoites, particularly in cases of amoebic 
liver abscess or systemic infection.
• Paromomycin (oral) is a luminal agent that acts in the intestines to eliminate amoeba cysts and 
trophozoites, which prevents recurrence and transmission.
What are their mechanisms of action?
• Metronidazole: Activation by parasite nitroreductase to nitroso free radicals (reactive oxygen species) 
that damage DNA and proteins
• Paromomycin: Protein synthesis inhibitor, binds 16S rRNA on the 30S ribosome.
What other organisms are susceptible to each of the drugs?
• Metronidazole is active against the protozoa Trichomonas vaginalis and Giardia lamblia as well 
Entamoeba histolytica.
• FYI: Metronidazole is also effective against obligate anaerobic gram -negative and gram-positive 
bacteria, including C. difficile.
• Paromomycin is active against luminal protoazoa G. lamblia and Cryptosporidium and Leishmania, 
which causes cutaneous, mucosal, and visceral infection.

What are the medical justifications for using two different drugs in the treatment of amoebiasis? What are the 
drugs described in this PowerPoint?
• Metronidazole is a tissue-acting drug to eliminate invasive trophozoites, particularly in cases of amoebic 
liver abscess or systemic infection.
• Paromomycin (oral) is a luminal agent that acts in the intestines to eliminate amoeba cysts and 
trophozoites, which prevents recurrence and transmission.
What are their mechanisms of action?
• Metronidazole: Activation by parasite nitroreductase to nitroso free radicals (reactive oxygen species) 
that damage DNA and proteins
• Paromomycin: Protein synthesis inhibitor, binds 16S rRNA on the 30S ribosome. 
What other organisms are susceptible to each of the drugs?
• Metronidazole is active against the protozoa Trichomonas vaginalis and Giardia lamblia as well 
Entamoeba histolytica.
• FYI: Metronidazole is also effective against obligate anaerobic gram -negativeand gram-positive bacteria, 
including C. difficile.
• Paromomycin is active against luminal protoazoa G. lamblia and Cryptosporidium and Leishmania, 
which causes cutaneous, mucosal, and visceral infection.
