=== PAGE 15 ===
Similarities between 
Sympathetic & Parasympathetic
• Both involve 2 neurons that synapse in a 
peripheral ganglion
• Both have cholinergic (release ACh) 
preganglionic neuron
• Both have myelinated preganglionic and 
unmyelinated postganglionic neuron 
• Both have nicotinic receptors on the 
postganglionic neuron
• Both have excitatory preganglionic 
neuron
• Both are visceromotor and autonomic 

=== PAGE 16 ===
• Length of the pre- and post-ganglionic neurons:
–
Symp. has short pre- and long post-ganglionic
–
Parasymp. has long pre- and short post-ganglionic
Differences between 
Sympathetic & Parasympathetic
• Postganglionic neurons:
– Parasympathetic are cholinergic (release ACh)  
– Sympathetic are adrenergic (release NE) with 2 
exceptions: sweat gland innervation (release 
Ach) and adrenal medulla (release NE and Epi)
• The effect depends on the receptors 
expressed in the target tissue, i.e. could be 
excitatory or inhibitory
• Preganglionic neuron body location in the CNS
–
Sympathetic is Thoraco-Lumbar
–
Parasympathetic is Cranio-Sacral

Carotid Sinus Massage
Indications:
•
Initial treatment of hemodynamically stable 
paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia
•
For the evaluation of syncope in patients older 
than 40 years of age
Contraindications:
•
Patients who are at risk for stroke due to 
carotid artery disease
•
Patients who have had a TIA or stroke within 
the preceding 3 months or who have clinically 
significant ipsilateral carotid stenosis or bruit
•
Complications from the previous massage, 
myocardial infarction in the preceding 3 
months, or any history of ventricular 
arrhythmia

=== PAGE 28 ===
Autonomic Dysreflexia
• Consequent to spinal cord injury, 
typically above T6
• Hyper-sensitivity of autonomic reflexes to 
sensory stimuli (i.e. autonomic spasticity)
• Sympathetic surge causing widespread 
vasoconstriction → peripheral arterial 
hypertension → detected by baroreceptors 
in the neck → parasympathetic slow heart 
rate
• Uncontrolled hypertension and 
bradycardia!
• Life-threatening
Stoelting's Anesthesia and Co-Existing Disease, 14, 309-320

=== PAGE 31 ===
Spastic bladder / Upper Motor Neuron bladder
Damage between the 
midthoracic spinal cord and the 
pontine micturition center. 
Vesicovesical reflex reemerges 
and becomes hyperactive 
External sphincter never gets a 
signal from the pontine 
micturition center telling it to 
relax → stay contracted
Detrusor has to contract really 
hard to produce enough pressure 
to overcome the contracted 
sphincter →urine can back up 
into the ureters to the kidneys
Nat. Rev. Neurosci. 2008; 9:453–66.

=== PAGE 32 ===
Atonic bladder / Lower Motor Neuron bladder
Lesions of the conus medullaris, 
cauda equina or sacral cord are a 
lot like LMN damage: 
Detrusor is unable to contract → 
bladder expands under relatively 
low pressure →bladder fills up but 
never completely empties 
External sphincters not 
contracted → small amount of 
urine may slowly leak out
Nat. Rev. Neurosci. 2008; 9:453–66.
