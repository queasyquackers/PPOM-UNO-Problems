WEEKLY EXAM GENERATION PROMPT v2 - ONE CURRICULUM WEEK, 40 ITEMS

One run produces ONE weekly exam file covering every lecture in one curriculum week. It is built on
question_generation_prompt_v6.txt: the same scope rules, high-yield gate, item-writing standards and
text rules, but a single mixed exam rather than two sets per lecture. Where this prompt and v6
disagree, this prompt wins for weekly exams.

  40 items per week.
  Every non-OMM lecture: about 60% first-order RECALL items and 40% second/third-order BOARD items,
    with at least one of each whenever the lecture has two or more items and supports an application.
  OMM lectures: RECALL items only.
  High-yield points only. Zero arithmetic. Every lab value given with its normal range.
  Target answering time: about 16 minutes (recall about 18 seconds, board about 33 seconds).

The weekly exam is a review tool. It must be fast and fair. The exams this replaces failed because
items were far too long and hard, chased low-yield details, required arithmetic, and gave lab values
without saying what was abnormal. Every rule below exists to prevent one of those failures.


**INPUTS (per lecture in the week)**
- The lecture PDF itself, not only extracted text: extraction drops bold, and some decks are
  image-only (CV11 carries text on 1 of 68 slides). Read image-only slides visually.
- The raw .srt transcript with timestamps, for dwell and verbal emphasis. Transcripts mis-transcribe
  technical terms; the slide spelling always wins.
- If a lecture has no transcript, say so in Part 1. Its points can qualify on slide signals alone
  (S1, S2 or S3 required); S5, S6 and S7 are unavailable.
- Confirm every deck by its title page. Lecture numbering restarts per course, so a number alone
  never identifies a deck.


**WHEN RULES CONFLICT**
Priority: (1) scope; (2) accuracy and one best answer; (3) the high-yield gate; (4) every other rule;
(5) counts and quotas. Miss a quota rather than leave scope, and report every shortfall in Part 1.
Answer balance is never waived.


**SCOPE (every item)**
- INTERSECTION RULE: test only concepts that are on a slide AND explained in the transcript (slide
  signals alone when no transcript exists, as above).
- THE LECTURE DECIDES THE ANSWER. The testing point, the key, and the reason each distractor is worse
  all come from that item's lecture. Outside knowledge may supply only scenery in a board vignette
  (typical history, vital-sign format, generic drug names) and connecting words in explanations.
- NO FUTURE CONCEPTS. Nothing from later courses or outside reading. Test at the depth taught.
- Do not key a simplified, absolute or apparently outdated lecture statement. Test the underlying
  principle or skip it, and report it.
- Nothing from a slide marked "not tested", "beyond the scope", "optional" or "for your interest",
  or verbally de-scoped by the lecturer.


**STEP 1 - HIGH-YIELD INVENTORY (per lecture)**
Classify every page: FRONT (title, objectives), CONTENT, BACK (references, feedback), QUESTION (the
deck's own practice item), or SKIPPED (no usable content, or de-scoped).

List testable points: one specific fact, rule, mechanism, relationship or clinical association per
point, zero to three per CONTENT page, most pages one or none. A point needs at least one signal:

  S1 OBJECTIVE     named on the learning-objectives slide - the specific concept, not merely the
                   broad topic an objective sits under
  S2 SUMMARY       restated on a summary, key points or take-home slide
  S3 DECK QUESTION what the deck's own practice question tests
  S4 BOLD          bold on the slide, if the deck passes the bold check
  S5 VERBAL        "really important", "remember this", "you need to know", "on the boards",
                   "high yield"
  S6 DWELL         the lecturer spends about 1.5x the deck's average time per content slide on it
  S7 REPEATED      the same specific claim restated at two separated points

- BOLD CHECK: if more than roughly 40% of the deck's body text is bold, bold is the body style;
  drop S4 for that deck. CV4, CV6, CV9, CV10, CV14 and CV15 are around 90%.
- NEGATION GUARD: read around every flag. "That's not particularly high yield" and "that's just for
  the USMLE" are exclusions. One announced sentence the lecturer never develops counts once.
- Never list dated absolutes, historical figures, years, eponym trivia, exact numbers that are not
  bold or an objective or repeated, or values readable only off a graph axis.

Score each point:
  YIELD 3  two or more signals including a slide signal (S1-S4) and a transcript signal (S5-S7)
  YIELD 2  two signals of any kind, OR S1, S2, S3 or S5 alone
  YIELD 1  one weaker signal: S4, S6 or S7 alone
ONLY YIELD 2 AND 3 POINTS ARE TESTABLE. An item on a yield-1 point is a defect even if it is
otherwise perfect. Prefer yield-3 points; use a yield-2 point only when a lecture's yield-3 points are
used up or would cluster on one slide.

Mark each point BOARD-SUITABLE when it supports a patient-centered application: findings that lead
to a mechanism, an expected lab or hemodynamic pattern, a predicted direction of change, an adverse
effect, a complication, or a next step. Pure definitions, histology identification, embryology naming
and technique steps are not. OMM points are never board-suitable.


**STEP 2 - ALLOCATION (40 items across the week)**
1. Weight each lecture by its count of yield 2-3 points. OMM lectures weigh 0.6 of that.
2. Give each lecture 2 items (fewer only if it has fewer yield 2-3 points).
3. Hand out the remaining items one at a time to the lecture with the highest weight / (items so far
   + 1), never past a cap of 7 per non-OMM lecture, 4 per OMM lecture, or the lecture's count of
   yield 2-3 points.
4. Within each non-OMM lecture with n items: board = round(0.4 x n), at least 1 when n is 2 or more
   and the lecture has a board-suitable point, never more than its board-suitable points, and always
   leaving at least one recall item. OMM lectures: board = 0.
5. If the week cannot reach 40 with yield 2-3 points, write fewer and report the shortfall.


====================================================================
RECALL ITEMS (first-order)
====================================================================

**FORM**
1. One fact, one question, no inference chain.
2. No patient: no age, sex, vital signs, labs or case. A bare clinical noun is allowed only when it IS
   the lecture content.
3. Default shape is a declarative subject with a short interrogative tail: "Intrinsic factor is
   secreted by which of the following cell types?" Reserve "Which of the following..." for genuine
   list discrimination.
4. Cover-the-options: a prepared student can state the answer with the options hidden.
5. Do not repeat a distinctive stem word or its root in the key unless every option uses it.

**WORD BUDGET (hard caps)**
  stem                  at most 25 words, target 15
  each option           at most 8 words, target 6
  correct explanation   "Correct." then 2 sentences, at most 45 words
  each incorrect expl.  at most 22 words INCLUDING the pivot sentence
  clinicalPearl         at most 25 words after "Key takeaway: "

**OPTIONS**
A noun phrase with an optional brief appositive, so all five share one silhouette: "Albumin, made by
the liver" / "Fibrinogen, a clotting protein" / "Immunoglobulin G antibody". Write distractors in the
key's register; the key must not be the only option that copies slide wording.

**EXPLANATIONS**
Correct option: "Correct." then the fact and the one contrast that matters.
Each incorrect option: "Incorrect." then one clause naming what the option actually is, then
"This would be the correct answer if the question had asked about ...". In a recall item this pivot
form is preferred: the identity of the wrong option is a second fact at almost no reading cost.

At least 40% of recall items should be answered by a direction, relationship or variable (increases,
shifts right, is inversely proportional to) rather than a name or value.


====================================================================
BOARD ITEMS (second/third-order)
====================================================================

**ITEM STANDARD**
1. Application: the stem requires at least one inference, from findings to an unstated state, then
   to the asked task. Keep the chain to one or two steps. This is review, not a trap.
2. Cover-the-options: answerable from the stem and lead-in with the options hidden.
3. Patient-centered: open with the patient. A data-only scenario (a described tracing or
   pressure-volume loop) is allowed only when interpreting that data is the testing point, and at
   most 2 per exam.
4. Hide what is asked: name what the patient has or was given when that is context, but never name
   the state the student must infer, and never label a finding with its interpretation.
5. Mechanism-first for pharmacology and pathology: describe the effect, adverse effect or finding
   without naming the drug class or disease being tested, unless the lecture's point IS the name.
6. Distractors fall to reasoning, not word matching. Include one fair strongest competitor that
   experts would rank second.
7. Accuracy: numbers and findings internally consistent and plausible (EDV = ESV + SV; no therapy the
   vignette's own data contraindicate).

**VIGNETTE FORMAT**
- Order, omitting what is not needed: age and sex; site of care; complaint and duration; history;
  examination; diagnostic studies; treatment and course.
- Vital signs in one sentence, and only the ones that matter: "Temperature is 37.2 C (99.0 F), pulse
  is 112/min, respirations are 24/min, and blood pressure is 96/60 mm Hg."
- Describe raw findings in words ("ST-segment elevation in leads II, III, and aVF"), because the app
  shows no images; never the interpretation when inferring it is the task.
- Generic drug names. Patients give a truthful history.

**LEAD-IN**
One closed question about this patient, ending in "?", usually with a superlative. Forbidden:
"Which statement...", EXCEPT, NOT, LEAST, two questions in one, "both X and Y", stems ending in ":".
Vary the task: mechanism or cause, expected finding or lab pattern, predicted direction of change,
adverse effect, complication, next step.

**WORD BUDGET (hard caps)**
  stem                  at most 65 words, target 50 - shorter is better
  each option           at most 12 words, target 1-6
  correct explanation   "Correct." then 3-4 sentences, at most 60 words
  each incorrect expl.  at most 35 words INCLUDING the pivot sentence
  clinicalPearl         at most 30 words after "Key takeaway: "

**OPTIONS**
Short parallel noun phrases, capitalized, no final period. One element per option: no "so", "since",
"because", "which" or "leading to" clauses; no semicolons or parentheses. Sets of findings (blood gas,
electrolyte, hemodynamic patterns) list the same variables in the same order and format in every
option. Numeric options share one format and units, in ascending order.

**EXPLANATIONS**
Correct option: "Correct." then the testing point, the chain from this vignette's findings to the
answer, and why the strongest competitor is worse, named by its content.
Each incorrect option: "Incorrect." then why it is wrong FOR THIS PATIENT citing the findings that make
it worse, then "This would be the correct answer if" naming a REAL CLINICAL SETTING or data pattern.
The "if the question had asked..." form is FORBIDDEN in board items, as are counterfactual definitions
and counterfactual identities: the pivot varies the patient, not the question.


====================================================================
RULES FOR BOTH ITEM TYPES
====================================================================

**LAB VALUES (stricter than NBME style, and non-negotiable)**
- Every lab value in a stem carries its normal range immediately after it: "a serum potassium of
  6.1 mEq/L (N=3.5-5.0)". This includes basic metabolic panel, CBC, blood gas values and pH. Vital
  signs need no range.
- Never label a value high or low in the stem, but the CORRECT explanation must say which given
  values are abnormal and what each abnormality indicates.
- Include a lab value only when it is needed to reach the answer.

**NUMBERS**
- ZERO arithmetic in any item. Never ask the student to calculate, estimate a ratio, or combine numbers.
- Recall stems contain fewer than three numbers.
- At most 3 recall items and at most 1 board item per exam are keyed on an exact number, and only on a
  threshold that is bold, an objective, or repeated by the lecturer.
- At most 4 board stems contain three or more numbers, not counting the age and "(N=...)" ranges.
- Permanently banned: percentage-guessing options, counting criteria, list positions, obscure
  eponyms, values readable only off a graph axis, years and historical figures.

**OPTIONS**
- Exactly 5, one category and one grammatical form, each directly answering the stem.
- No absolute terms (always, never, only, must, cannot, completely, purely) in any option.
- No "None of the above" or "All of the above", no opposite pairs, no collectively exhaustive sets
  (increased / decreased / no change), no two options meaning the same thing.
- Every distractor is a real thing from the lecture or a common misconception, never filler.

**VOICE AND TEXT**
- Standalone: never refer to another item ("the same patient", "described earlier"). Review modes
  shuffle items from many exams together.
- No classroom or source framing anywhere: no students, instructors or lecturers, no "the lecture
  said", no "according to".
- Plain ASCII only in every field: write "C", "beta-1", "alpha", "mcg", "->", "-", "x", "at least",
  "PCO2", "HCO3-". Never the "<" character; the app injects text as HTML, so write "less than".

**CATEGORY**
Recall items "Topic: Subtopic"; board items "System: Topic". Reuse strings within a lecture: one or
two distinct categories per item type per lecture. The performance summary groups by exact string.

**CLINICAL PEARL**
Starts with the literal "Key takeaway: " and states the item's testing point as one standalone,
transferable rule. It uses only facts already in that item's correct explanation.


====================================================================
EXAM-LEVEL RULES
====================================================================

**ORDER**
Interleave the lectures round-robin, taking each lecture's items in slide order, so recall and board
items mix and no lecture arrives as a block.

**ANSWER BALANCE**
- Position: exactly 8 keys per letter A-E. No letter keyed three times in a row, and no repeating
  cycle such as A, B, C, D, E, A, B, C.
- Length is the character count of the option text. The key is "longest" when it has more
  characters than every distractor and "shortest" when it has fewer than every distractor; ties count
  as neither. Across the 40 items the key must be longest in 5-11 AND shortest in 5-11. A key that is
  never longest is itself a tell.
- Method: write all five options to the form rules, then count. If a count falls outside 5-11,
  rephrase within the form rules; never pad or clip with filler. After rephrasing, recheck that no
  distractor became defensible and that meaning did not drift.

**NO LEAKAGE**
- No option string appears in two different items; one item's key is never another's distractor.
- No two items share three or more option strings.

**PER-LECTURE BREAKDOWN**
The summary screen scores a finished weekly exam by lecture, keyed on the lecture id in lectureSource.
Every item's lectureSource must name its own lecture exactly, or a missed item is reported under the
wrong lecture.


**SCHEMA**
Every item carries, in this order: id, category, questionText, options (exactly 5, each {text,
explanation}), correctAnswerIndex, clinicalPearl, lectureSource, pdfLecture, pdfPage, pdfQuote,
itemType.
- id: sequential integer from 1.
- correctAnswerIndex (0-4) points to the option whose explanation starts "Correct.".
- lectureSource: "CV7: Lecture Title", identical for every item from that lecture.
- pdfLecture: the scripts/pdf_mapping.js key for that lecture, on every item.
- pdfPage: the page, as a PDF viewer counts, where the keyed fact is PRIMARILY TAUGHT - not an
  objectives or summary page when a teaching page exists, and not a question page.
- pdfQuote: 2-10 words copied verbatim from that page, unique on it; prefer the slide title. Avoid
  spans with subscripts or superscripts. On an image-only page with no extractable text, use "" and
  report it. The app never renders pdfQuote; it is the audit anchor.
- itemType: "recall" or "board".
- Strict JSON inside the array: double-quoted keys and strings, no trailing commas, no comments.


====================================================================
OUTPUT FORMAT
====================================================================

**PART 1: PLANNING** (all of it, before any code)

1. Lectures - one row per lecture: id | title | PDF filename | title page verified | transcript
   (timestamped / text only / none) | bold density and whether S4 was used | OMM yes or no.
2. Inventory summary - one row per lecture: content pages | skipped pages with reasons | yield-3,
   yield-2 and board-suitable point counts.
3. Allocation - one row per lecture: items | recall | board, with the arithmetic from Step 2, and any
   shortfall.
4. Item table - one row per item: id | lecture | itemType | pdfPage | testing point | signals |
   verbatim evidence (a slide phrase or a 6-20 word transcript phrase) | planned key letter.
5. Tallies:
     items (40); recall and board totals; per lecture recall/board, OMM board = 0;
     keys per letter (8 each); longest run of one letter (at most 2); key-longest (5-11);
     key-shortest (5-11); longest recall stem (25 words) and option (8); longest board stem (65) and
     option (12); longest correct explanation (recall 45, board 60); longest incorrect explanation
     (recall 22, board 35); arithmetic items (0); lab values without "(N=...)" (0); recall stems with
     3+ numbers (0); number-keyed items (recall 3, board 1); board stems with 3+ numbers (4);
     data-only board items (2); shared option strings (0); ASCII only; no "<"; every explanation
     starts "Correct." or "Incorrect."; every incorrect explanation contains "This would be the
     correct answer if"; no board pivot uses "if the question"; every pearl starts "Key takeaway: ".
6. Scope report - skipped slides and why; quotas missed and why; simplified or outdated statements
   and how each was handled; image-only pages used.

**PART 3: REGISTRATION**
- Replace the existing file of the same name. Keep the existing config.js entry unchanged, e.g.
  { name: "Cardio-WEEK 1 CUMULATIVE EXAM (CV1-10)", data: window.Test_Cumulative_CPR_Wk1 }.
  The test name is the permanent localStorage progress key: renaming it wipes students' scores.
- Changing an exam's question count resets saved progress for that exam automatically (app.js
  discards saved answers when the count differs), so a rebuilt 40-item exam never shows old answers
  against new questions. If a rebuild keeps the SAME count, saved answers would land on the new
  questions: change the count or clear that exam's progress.
- index.html already loads the file. Bump the file's query-string version if one is present.
